Policy for Using AI/GenAI Tools in Research and Work: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Getting Started & General Use

I’d like to use GenAI in my work; where should I start?

You should first investigate how GenAI is being applied in your specific discipline, as standards often vary by field. When planning research, incorporate GenAI use into your funding proposals and ethics applications, especially if personal data is involved. To ensure data security and reproducibility, the Institute recommends running tools locally or using models from community platforms like Hugging Face when possible.

What is the difference between "substantive" and "non-substantive" use?
  • Substantive Use: Includes interpreting data, undertaking literature reviews, identifying research gaps, or generating hypotheses. This requires formal documentation in research notebooks.
  • Non-Substantive Use: Includes minor editing, reducing word count, improving readability, or converting documents to slide decks. These tasks generally do not require a formal declaration unless they incorporate work authored by others.
In what ways can professional staff use GenAI to improve efficiency?

Professional staff can use GenAI for substantive tasks such as summarizing long reports, drafting minutes from transcripts, creating policy briefs, or reformatting data to identify process inefficiencies. Non-substantive tasks include proofreading web content, rephrasing emails, and workshop planning.

Research Integrity & Ethics

Can I use GenAI to write my abstract or edit my manuscript?
  • Usage: Using GenAI for drafting your own work is generally considered non-substantive use if the tasks involve summarizing, reducing word count, or improving readability.
  • Caution: You must ensure that any GenAI use does not incorporate third-party work that could infringe on copyright.
  • Integrity: Authors remain fully accountable for the accuracy and originality of the final text.
Can I use GenAI to develop my research proposal or check references?
  • External Funding: If applying to US federal agencies (e.g., NIH, NSF, DOE) or private foundations, you must strictly follow their specific GenAI disclosure and usage policies.
  • Internal Funding: Use of GenAI might be permitted for internal Institute grant applications, provided that all substantive use — such as analyzing data or identifying research gaps — is clearly declared in the proposal.
  • Verification: Always verify AI-generated citations, as GenAI tools frequently produce "hallucinations" or fabricated references.
Can I use GenAI to edit research images?

No. Where images are research results used to evidence findings, using GenAI to edit them is inappropriate and constitutes a breach of good research conduct.

What should I do to ensure the quality of AI-generated outputs?

You must validate GenAI outputs with the same rigor you apply to work from other researchers or instrument measurements. While "iterative prompting" (refining prompts) can improve results, remain aware that outputs may eventually converge too closely to your line of inquiry, potentially introducing bias.

How should I document and declare the use of GenAI? Substantive use must be documented in research notebooks with a complete transcript of the output, the date, and the version of the tool used. When sharing research, your declaration should include:
  • Name and version of the tool (e.g., Claude, ChatGPT-4).
  • The publisher (e.g., Anthropic, OpenAI).
  • A brief description of the context of use.

Funding, Publishing & Collaboration

Can I use GenAI in grant applications or interviews?

If applying for external funding (e.g., NIH, NSF, DOE), you must comply with their specific AI policies. For internal Institute funds, GenAI is permitted for development if declared. However, GenAI should not be used to generate responses during live interviews or to rank proposals for funding decisions.

What are the rules for publishing research where GenAI was used?

Most publishers require a clear explanation of GenAI use. Note that GenAI tools cannot be listed as authors. You remain fully accountable for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the research. If you discover an AI-generated error after publication, contact the journal editor immediately to issue a correction or retraction.

My research involves outside collaborators. Do they need to comply?

You are encouraged to share the Institute's policy with all collaborators. If your research involves University facilities or personal data subject to our research ethics policy, collaborators must comply with these institutional standards.

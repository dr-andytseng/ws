Policy for Using AI/GenAI Tools in Research and Work

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is a rapidly evolving technology that offers significant opportunities and, as with all new technologies, comes with inherent risks. The Whitehead Institute for Biomedical Research (the “Institute”) wishes to enable and support the safe, ethical, and productive use of AI/GenAI across all Institute activities.

The aims of this policy are to ensure the responsible and ethical use of AI/GenAI in research and work processes without putting institutional, personal, or proprietary information at risk. This policy is supported by, and should be read alongside, the Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) which provide good practice advice and actionable guidance regarding the use of AI/GenAI tools. The Institute promotes adherence to relevant funder and publisher policies and maintains its commitment to scientific integrity. Users are advised to interpret the use of AI/GenAI in the context of research practice standards on transparency, reproducibility, and security.

Use of AI/GenAI at the Institute is within the scope of research and professional conduct. Actions found to be in potential breach will be reviewed in line with the Institute’s policies on ethics and research conduct.

Scope of the Policy

This policy applies to all Institute personnel, including employees, faculty, fellows, academic appointees, contractors, consultants, and anyone using AI as part of Institute research, projects, and activities. The policy encompasses the substantive use of GenAI in the research lifecycle, including applying for funding, conducting research, and disseminating outputs.

Substantive Use is Defined as:

Using GenAI in work to: Interpret and analyze data (including audio and image files) or texts. Undertake a literature review or translation. Identify research gaps or generate hypotheses. Formulate research aims or assist with generating ideas. Generate code and synthetic data. Undertake transcription of interviews or meetings to save time and effort. Produce documents using other people’s work or from transcripts and recordings.

Exclusions (Non-Substantive Use):

Substantive use excludes tasks such as: Assisting non-native speakers with translation. Improving one’s own standard of English or a foreign language. Formatting documents. Transforming spoken language to written language for users who face barriers to writing.

This policy applies to all researchers, i.e. members of the Institute, including staff, students who are conducting research, and those who are not members of the Institute but who are conducting research on Institute premises (e.g., remote collaborators), or using Institute facilities or funding for their research, such as visiting staff, fellows, students and contractors. It also applies to Professional staff, Institute staff who in some capacity support research, e.g. research facilitators, technicians, IT support staff, library staff, and administrative support staff.

See FAQ & glossary for definitions of other terms used in this policy.
